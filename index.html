Objection Handling
12 most likely objections · Track A and Track B · Prepared responses · Memorize the first 6
Rule Before You Read This

Never argue with an objection. Acknowledge → Redirect → Evidence. The goal is not to win the argument. The goal is to keep the conversation moving toward a next step. An objection is a buying signal — it means they're engaged enough to push back.

Track B — Advertiser Objections
Objection 1: "We don't have a media budget right now."

"Understood — I'm not here to close a deal today anyway. I want to understand your buyer and whether our audience overlap makes sense. If it does, budgets tend to find their way. Can I ask — who's the aircraft buyer segment you most want to reach in 2026?"

Why it works: Removes pressure, re-opens diagnostic mode, gets them talking about their problem instead of their budget.

Objection 2: "We already advertise with Controller.com / AIN / Aviation Week."

"Those are awareness plays. GlobalAir.com is intent. When someone searches 'Citation for sale' or 'FBO near [airport]' and lands on our site, they're not casually reading — they're researching. Your ads on our platform reach buyers in decision mode, not browsing mode. We're not competing with those — we're covering a different moment in the buyer journey."

Why it works: Repositions the conversation from share of spend to stage of funnel. They want both — you just have to frame it correctly.

Objection 3: "We need to measure ROI — how do we track this?"

"Good question — and honestly the right one to ask. We can track click-through to your site, UTM-tagged traffic from our newsletter, and inquiry volume from sponsored content. What's your current conversion point — form submission, phone call, demo request? We build attribution around your KPI, not ours."

Why it works: Shows operational sophistication. Most aviation media publishers give vague answers to this — specificity differentiates.

Objection 4: "Your audience is aircraft buyers, but we sell to flight departments / operators."

"Aircraft buyers become operators. And operators research on GlobalAir.com too — for FBO selection, spec sheets, market comparisons. We have 201,000 organic keywords and 4.75 million annual visits. That's not all first-time buyers — that's the entire aviation operator community researching in one place."

Why it works: Expands the audience framing beyond 'buyer.' Operators, fleet managers, and dispatchers all use GlobalAir.com.

Objection 5: "We're not sure this is the right conference for that conversation."

"Fair — and I'm not trying to close a deal here. I wanted to introduce ourselves because the people in this room use our platform daily. Schedulers and dispatchers search GlobalAir.com to research FBOs, compare fuel prices, and look at aircraft specs. Your buyers are our users. That's worth a 20-minute call after the show."

Why it works: Validates the objection, reframes the goal as a call not a close, leaves with a specific next step.

Objection 6: "Can you send me a media kit?"

"I can — and I will. But before I do, can I ask one question? A media kit is generic. What I'd rather send you is a one-page hypothesis on what a GlobalAir.com package would look like specifically for [Company] — based on what you've told me today. That's more useful than a rate card. Is that okay?"

Why it works: Prevents the media kit from becoming a dead-end. Pivots to a custom proposal that's harder to ignore.

Track A — Data Partnership Objections
Objection 7: "We already work with JETNET."

"JETNET tracks aircraft. We track buyers. They can tell you what aircraft exists — we can tell you who's about to buy it. A JETNET relationship and a GlobalAir.com data partnership aren't competitive — they're additive. JETNET gives you the asset side of the equation. We give you the demand side. Together that's the full picture."

Why it works: Positions as complementary, not competitive. Removes the either/or framing immediately.

Objection 8: "We're not sure about data sharing / privacy / GDPR."

"That's the right instinct and we've thought about it. The model we're exploring is anonymized, aggregated signals — not raw PII. We're not asking for your customer records. We're asking whether there's a way to understand operator behavior patterns at the category level without exposing individual identity. That's a legal framework question, not a no. Let's get the right people on a call."

Why it works: Immediately reframes scope. Shows you've already thought through their concern. Moves to next step rather than getting stuck.

Objection 9: "We're a small team — we don't have bandwidth for a partnership build."

"I hear that — and I'm not proposing a 12-month integration project. The simplest version of this is a clean API connection and a data use agreement. You give us nothing new to build. We plug into what you already have. What would a minimal viable version of this look like from your side?"

Why it works: Reduces scope to MVP. Gets them thinking about solutions, not barriers. Shifts from objection to design conversation.

Objection 10: "What's the revenue share model?"

"We haven't locked that down because it depends on what you bring to the table. The framework we're exploring is: you provide API access and data rights on anonymized signals, we provide distribution and brand. Revenue share on anything generated through GlobalAir.com distribution, plus joint ownership of the data insights we produce together. The exact split is something we'd negotiate based on the value of what each side brings."

Why it works: Shows you have a framework without boxing yourself in. Invites them into the design conversation rather than presenting a take-it-or-leave-it structure.

Objection 11: "Our investors / parent company would need to approve this."

"Of course — and I'm not expecting a handshake deal here. What I want to understand is whether you see the strategic logic. If you do, we can build the business case together for whoever needs to approve it. What would make this easy for you to present internally?"

Why it works: Recruits them as an internal champion rather than treating them as a gatekeeper. Gives you insight into the approval process.

Objection 12: "We already have partnerships — we're not looking for more right now."

"Understood — and I respect that. I'll just ask one question and then I'll leave you alone: do any of your current partners give you access to the buyer demand side of aviation — who's in market, what they're researching, what they're about to purchase? If yes, I'll walk away. If no, that's the gap we fill. And nobody else fills it."

Why it works: One pointed question that either surfaces a genuine need or gives you a clean exit. No hard sell. Respects their time.
